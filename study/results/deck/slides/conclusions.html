Conclusions

On the Pi 5, spend an INT8 budget on input resolution. Pruning does not pay.

A · INT8 wins, pruning loses

INT8 pairs +0.0044 to +0.0145 AP-small; pruned pairs −0.0027 to −0.0042

B · NMS is about 1 ms

At the deployment threshold; the NMS-free head does not pay for itself on the Pi 5

C · YOLO26s: a little better, a little slower

Its latency gap grows to +9.0 % at 1280 on NCNN

D · The recipe sets INT8’s cost

1.38 to 1.51× faster; per-channel costs 2 to 10 % of AP, stock up to 20 %

Small objects lose 1.13 to 1.47× more than AP: real, but under the 2× bar.

Restate the reasoning chain. The budget is measured latency. INT8 buys it cheaply (1.38 to 1.51× for 2 to 10 % of AP); pruning buys more speed (about 1.5 to 1.6×) but at about 13 to 14 % of AP at 960. Per unit of speed-up, INT8 is the cheapest way to buy latency, a lower input resolution is dearer, and pruning is dearest. The practical rule for a drone on a Pi-class CPU: quantize with per-channel weights, run NCNN if you can, raise the input resolution with what you save, and do not expect NMS or the model choice to be the lever. On the consumer-grade phone the INT8 step is bigger (+0.0355 AP-small), but that result is provisional.
