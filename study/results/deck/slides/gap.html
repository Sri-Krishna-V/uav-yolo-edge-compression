The gap, said honestly. Pruning and efficiency surveys show that FLOPs and parameter counts do not translate into latency on a device: the engine and the tensor shapes dominate [6, 7, 8]. Aerial detection papers mostly report FLOPs or desktop-GPU frames per second [9, 23, 24]. Spending more pixels on small objects is known to help, through tiling [4] and resolution scaling [5]. The idea of spending an INT8 saving on resolution is not new: Plastiras et al. [10] and Vandersteegen et al. [11] did it on embedded GPUs, but without holding measured latency fixed, without AP-small, and without pruning. Compression hurting small objects more than large ones has been reported for INT8 on VisDrone [21] and for pruning [22]; I-YOLOv11n [23] asserts it for pruning without isolating it. Our novelty check (17 October, about 70 searches) found no controlled, equal-latency study on CPU-class platforms with AP-small. What is new here is the controlled design, the pruning arm, the YOLO11s against YOLO26s comparison and the CPU-class platforms; not the idea.
