Pair rule: same platform, model, runtime and head; median latencies within 10 % of the smaller. Distilled models never pair: they save no latency.

We read AP-small of the compressed cell minus the baseline’s. 12 pairs: 11 on the Pi 5, 1 on the phone. Closest prior designs: [10, 11].

YOLO11s · ONNX Runtime · Raspberry Pi 5 · tables/equal_latency.csv

This is the reasoning behind the main result. A compressed model is useful only if, at the same latency, it beats what you could have had by running a plain model at a smaller input resolution. So we pair cells by measured latency, never by FLOPs or parameters. The 10 % rule is issue #8’s. Example (YOLO11s, ONNX Runtime, Pi 5): B1 INT8 at 960 runs in 617.2 ms; B1 FP32 at 800 runs in 629.9 ms; they are 2.1 % apart, so they form a pair. The INT8 cell saves 32 % of the FP32 latency at 960 (617.2 against 909.7 ms). Distilled cells run at the baseline’s latency (388.1 against 389.8 ms) and so never enter a pair.
