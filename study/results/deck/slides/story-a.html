This is the answer to the main question. Each bar is one equal-latency pair: a compressed model at a larger input resolution against the plain model at a smaller one, both within 10 % in measured latency on the Pi 5. The INT8 pairs gain AP-small in all five cases, by 3 to 16 % of the plain model’s AP-small (+0.0044 to +0.0145). The gain shrinks as resolution grows: +16 % from 640 to 800, +3 to 4 % from 960 to 1120. Pruning buys the same step from 800 to 960 but loses 2 to 4 % on every runtime. So the budget is worth spending on resolution with INT8, and not with pruning. Two honest qualifications. First, this holds for per-channel INT8: with the stock recipe YOLO26s’s pairs gave +0.0008 and −0.0140 (−10 %). Second, the six pruned bars are two models on three runtimes, and ONNX Runtime and OpenVINO give identical accuracy, so they are two accuracy results; the pruned loss holds on the clean OpenVINO and NCNN pairs too. Some pairs compare a checkpoint at an input resolution it was not trained at (800 and 1120). Caveats: single seed; YOLO11s’s three seeds at 640 spread 0.0027 AP-small, so the two smallest gains (+0.0044, +0.0049) are only about 1.6 to 1.8× that; the ONNX Runtime FP32 baselines throttled, so their latencies may read a few percent high, which moves how far apart each pair is (RESULTS.md, Result A caveats); intervals come from #48.
