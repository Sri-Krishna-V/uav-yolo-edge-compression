NCNN FP16’s speed-up over ONNX Runtime FP32: the runtime beats INT8

fig_d_int8 · tables/result_d_int8.csv · tables/result_d_runtimes.csv

Claim: on the Pi 5, INT8 does give a speed-up, the same with either recipe (1.38 to 1.51×); how much accuracy it costs depends on the recipe; and a different runtime beats it outright. Per-channel AP cost: 9.5, 6.3, 6.4, 4.8, 2.2 % for YOLO11s and 7.6, 6.5, 4.7, 5.3 % for YOLO26s. The stock recipe cost YOLO26s 14.1 to 20.3 % of AP, which is why per-channel weights are the matrix’s INT8. The 1120 rows (§) set a session 7 INT8 cell against a session 6 FP32 cell that ran about 6 % slow, so their per-channel speed-up reads high. The FP32 side throttled, so the speed-ups are if anything high. Runtime: NCNN FP16 is 1.98 to 2.28× faster than ONNX Runtime FP32 and 28 to 37 % faster than ONNX Runtime INT8, with higher AP; OpenVINO FP32 is 1.12 to 1.33× faster. NCNN’s gain bundles the runtime and its FP16 arithmetic, which cannot be separated here. On the consumer-grade phone (provisional), INT8 is 2.01× and 1.93× faster.
