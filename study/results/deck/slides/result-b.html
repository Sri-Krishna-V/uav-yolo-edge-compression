Claim: at the deployment threshold NMS is a rounding error on the Pi 5, so the NMS-free head’s selling point does not show up in latency. The one-to-many head’s post-processing at 0.25 is 1.07 to 1.12 ms across the four YOLO26s pairs (0.29 to 0.40 % of latency); the NMS-free head’s is 0.15 to 0.18 ms. At the 0.001 evaluation threshold NMS takes 14.8 to 15.6 ms, but that is the scoring pass, not deployment. In the figure’s cell (per-channel INT8), NMS at 0.25 grows from 0.56 ms in the sparsest images (1 to 12 objects) to 2.22 ms in the densest (88 to 461 objects): still under 1 % of a 277 ms latency. The NMS-free head’s network is slower, so its end-to-end latency is +2.1 ms on this clean INT8 pair, and its AP is 0.0056 lower. Its post-processing time sits at the timer’s resolution, so it is drawn as one pooled band. Sources: tables/result_b_heads.csv, tables/result_b_bins.csv.
