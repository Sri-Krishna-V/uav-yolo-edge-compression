Result D · the INT8 recipe

How INT8 is calibrated decides its cost: the default recipe cost YOLO26s a fifth of its AP

Stock: one weight scale per layer. Per-channel: one per output channel [14, 15].

YOLO26s’s box branch has channels whose ranges differ 4 to 10×; one scale rounds the small ones away (#41).

Same speed, either recipe: 1.4 to 1.5×.

s09_recipe · tables/result_d_int8.csv · ONNX Runtime INT8 at 640, Raspberry Pi 5

Result D, part two. Our first INT8 cells used ONNX Runtime’s default, one weight scale per layer. YOLO26s lost 20.3 % of its AP that way, against 10.3 % for YOLO11s, which first looked like a property of the model. Issue #41 traced it to YOLO26s’s 32-channel box branch, whose channel ranges differ 4 to 10 times, so one shared scale rounds the small channels away. Per-channel weight scales, the standard remedy in the quantization literature [14, 15], with the same calibration data and the same speed, cut the loss to 7.6 %. YOLO11s barely changed (9.5 %). The lesson for the paper: the quantization recipe, not the architecture’s headline features, decided YOLO26s’s INT8 cost; and every INT8 result we report uses per-channel weights. The speed-up is the same either way: 1.38 to 1.51× on the Pi 5. The remaining 2 to 10 % loss is not examined. Source: tables/result_d_int8.csv; #41.
