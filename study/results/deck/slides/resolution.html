Compression saves latency. Could it pay for the pixels?

s02_resolution · cells.csv, ONNX Runtime FP32 baselines, Raspberry Pi 5

The natural lever for small objects is pixels. Taking the plain models from input resolution 640 to 1280 raises AP-small by 79 % (YOLO11s) and 67 % (YOLO26s), against 33 to 34 % for medium objects and 22 to 25 % for large ones. But it costs 4.35 times the latency on the Pi 5 (389.8 to 1,695.1 ms for YOLO11s). This is the tension the study is about: compression saves latency, and we ask whether that saving is best spent on a larger input resolution. Prior work uses the same lever through tiling (SAHI [4]) and compound scaling (EfficientDet [5]). Be precise if asked: the gain is relative. In absolute AP points small objects gain the least (YOLO11s: +0.072 small, +0.096 medium, +0.093 large), because they start from far lower AP (0.090 against 0.284 and 0.420). The 1280 cell is a checkpoint trained at 1280, not the 640 model run at a larger input. Caveat: these ONNX Runtime FP32 baselines were flagged throttled on the Pi 5, so the latency ratio may be slightly high; the accuracy gains are not affected. Source: cells.csv.
