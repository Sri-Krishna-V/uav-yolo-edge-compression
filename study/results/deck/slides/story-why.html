Per-channel INT8 buys speed at about half the small-object cost of the alternatives

Same starting point: the plain model at 960 on ONNX Runtime FP32.

All three buy 1.4 to 1.6× speed. INT8 loses 6 to 8 % of AP-small; dropping to 800 loses 15 %; pruning 17 to 18 %.

So INT8 leaves room for more pixels; pruning does not.

This slide explains result A. From the same plain model at 960, there are three ways to make it 1.4 to 1.6 times faster on the Pi 5: quantize it to INT8 (1.47× for YOLO11s), run it at the smaller input resolution 800 (1.44×), or prune it (1.58×). INT8 costs 7.8 % of AP-small, the smaller input 14.8 %, pruning 17.9 %; YOLO26s agrees (5.9, 14.9 and 16.9 %). INT8 is the cheapest speed per point of small-object accuracy, so its saving can be spent on resolution and still come out ahead; pruning costs more than simply lowering the resolution, so spending its saving on resolution cannot win. Caveats: the speed-ups are against throttled FP32 baselines, so they may be slightly high, equally for all three; the 800 cell is the 960 checkpoint run at a resolution it was not trained at, so part of its loss is that mismatch. The percentages are relative: INT8’s −8 % is 0.1365 to 0.1259, about one AP-small point. On the unthrottled OpenVINO runtime the 800 and pruned speed-ups are about 1.5×, so the ranking holds. With the stock INT8 recipe YOLO26s loses 14 %, as much as dropping to 800: the claim is about per-channel INT8. Source: cells.csv.
