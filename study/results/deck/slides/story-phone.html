The same question on a consumer-grade phone. INT8 is about twice as fast there (2.01× for YOLO11s, 1.93× for YOLO26s at 640) against about 1.4× on the Pi 5, so INT8 can afford a bigger resolution step: INT8 at 960 lands near the plain model at 640 (733.5 against 675.2 ms, 8.6 % apart) and gains 0.0355 AP-small, 39 % of the plain model’s. Read it as “a bigger step was affordable”, not as “the phone is better at INT8”. Everything about the phone’s latency is provisional: it has no clock lock, its drift checks failed by 4.9 and 3.4 %, and 8.6 % plus 5 % drift exceeds the 10 % pair rule. Phone sessions 3 and 4 add more pairs. Accuracy on the phone matches the Pi 5’s to four digits. Do not let the chart suggest the phone is faster: it is slower in absolute terms (FP32 675 against 390 ms; INT8 336 against 272 ms); only its INT8 ratio is larger. The Pi 5’s FP32 baselines throttled, which inflates the Pi 5 ratio, so the true gap is if anything larger. Sources: result_d_int8.csv per_channel_speedup; equal_latency.csv, platform m34.
