Claim: on the Pi 5, a compression budget spent on input resolution pays off for INT8 and not for pruning. Point at panel (c) first, then at one connector in panel (a). The five INT8 pairs (per-channel): YOLO11s INT8 at 800 vs FP32 at 640: +0.0145, 6.8 % apart; INT8 at 960 vs FP32 at 800: +0.0096, 2.1 %; INT8 at 1120 vs FP32 at 960: +0.0049, 9.3 %; YOLO26s INT8 at 960 vs FP32 at 800: +0.0125, 5.6 %; INT8 at 1120 vs FP32 at 960: +0.0044, 5.2 %. Relative gains are 3.2 to 16.1 % of the baseline’s AP-small. Why INT8 wins: at 960, from the same ONNX Runtime FP32 baseline, INT8 per-channel buys 1.47× for −6.4 % AP; dropping to 800 buys 1.44× for −8.4 %; pruning buys 1.58× for −14.1 % (YOLO11s; YOLO26s agrees: 1.43× −4.7 %, 1.51× −7.9 %, 1.62× −12.8 %). INT8 is the cheapest latency per point of AP lost. The stock INT8 recipe gave YOLO26s a tie and a loss (+0.0008, −0.0140), which is why the per-channel fix matters.
